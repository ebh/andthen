# Copyright terms: equal shares

Version 1 · Effective 2026-11-09

## In short

- You keep the copyright in the passages you write.
- Every other passage author and every other writer of the story, now or later, may use the whole story, including your passages. That stays true if you leave the story or delete your account.
- Using the story for non-commercial purposes is free.
- Any passage author or writer may publish the whole story. If it earns money, the net proceeds are split equally between the passage authors who hold an account when the money is paid.
- Every published copy names every passage author by display name.
- andthen.page holds no money and is not a party to these terms. They are a promise between the passage authors and writers of a story.

## What these terms are

These terms are the copyright model called "equal shares". You accept them for each story you start or join as a writer. They are an agreement between you and every other passage author and writer of that story, now and in the future.

The operator of the app is andthen.page. You can reach it at support@andthen.page. These terms do not make andthen.page a party to the agreement between passage authors and writers. What you give andthen.page permission to do with your text is set out in the terms of service, not here.

## Words used here

- **Story:** the text that a group writes together, in turns.
- **Passage:** a section of a story's text.
- **Writer:** a participant in a story who may write passages.
- **Passage author:** a person who wrote at least one passage in the story. This includes a person who has since left the story.
- **Copyright model:** a set of copyright terms that a story can be written under. This one is "equal shares".
- **Account:** the sign-in that a person can hold in the app.
- **Display name:** the name shown beside a writer's passages in a story.
- **Non-commercial use:** use that is not meant to earn money, such as a blog post, reading the story aloud or sharing it with friends.
- **Publish:** to use the story commercially. This includes adaptations, such as a sequel, an audiobook, or a stage or screen version.

## Your passages

You keep the copyright in every passage you write. These terms do not transfer ownership of anything.

A passage that you send from an AI suggestion counts as written by you. You are its passage author, and these terms apply to it as they do to any other passage you write.

## The licence you give

You give every other passage author and every other writer of the story, now or later, a licence to use the whole story, which means all of its passages, including yours. The licence is:

- non-exclusive, so you may keep using your own passages in any way you like;
- irrevocable, so you cannot take it back;
- worldwide; and
- royalty-free for non-commercial use.

The licence survives. It stays in force after you leave the story, and after you delete your account. Leaving the story does not end the licence you give or the licence you receive.

Commercial use is also covered by the licence, but only on the terms in the next two sections.

## Non-commercial use

Any passage author or writer may use the story for non-commercial purposes. This is free. It owes no share to anyone.

## Publishing

Any passage author or writer may publish the whole story without asking the others.

To earn money from the story, the publisher must hold an account. A writer without an account may not earn money from the story.

When the story earns money, the net proceeds are split equally per head among the passage authors who hold an account when the proceeds are paid. Each of them gets the same share, however much each of them wrote.

- **Net proceeds** are the money actually received, minus the reasonable, documented direct costs of publishing.
- **A deleted account gets no share.** A passage author who has deleted their account has no share in proceeds paid after that.
- **A passage author without an account gets no share until they hold one.** They then share only in proceeds paid after that time.
- **The publisher is a passage author too.** If the publisher wrote a passage and holds an account, they take a share on the same terms as everyone else.

The publisher must offer each entitled passage author their share. The publisher must also keep records of the money received, the costs deducted and the shares paid, and must show them to a passage author who asks.

## Publishers and deals

A passage author or writer may license a company or person to publish the story, but only non-exclusively. No deal may stop another passage author or writer from using the story as these terms allow.

## Credit

Every published copy of the story must name every passage author by display name. This includes passage authors who have left the story or deleted their account.

## Who has authorship

Only passage authors have authorship of the story. A person who takes part in a story in any other way, now or in future, for example by suggesting changes, gains no authorship and no share of any proceeds.

## Moral rights

As far as the law allows, you consent to:

- the story being used, and your passages being combined with the passages of other passage authors, in the ways these terms describe; and
- being credited by your display name instead of your legal name.

## Leaving and rejoining

Leaving a story does not end these terms for the passages you wrote. If a new version of these terms is introduced, you must accept it before it applies to you.

## What andthen.page does not do

- andthen.page holds no money.
- andthen.page does not check sales, earnings, costs or payments.
- andthen.page does not pass messages or money between writers.
- andthen.page is not a party to any dispute between writers.

The display name beside your passages is how others know you. A publisher has to find a way to reach you without help from andthen.page.
